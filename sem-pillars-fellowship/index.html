Aim

To spend a structured year developing my clinical and academic expertise across the three domains of Sport, Exercise & Musculoskeletal Medicine (SEM), being team and athlete care, musculoskeletal medicine, and exercise medicine, with colleagues who deliver quality and expertise in these domains across a range of contexts.

01

Team and athlete care

Athlete health systems, injury and illness prevention, and the safe delivery of sporting events.

02

Musculoskeletal medicine

Sports injury and trauma across the life course, including children, young people and people with disability.

03

Exercise medicine

Physical activity, obesity and cardiometabolic health, joint preservation, and population services.

Summary

My practice is strongest where orthopaedic surgery and elite sport meet, and thinner elsewhere. Very few clinicians practise across all three domains, and no single centre teaches all three well, which is why this is proposed as a series of short attachments rather than one long one. Each is paired with work already under way through STRIKERS Elite Sport Group, and the fellowship would facilitate collaborations that are multidisciplinary, multifocal and multicentre.

The timing is relevant, since BASEM and the Faculty of Sport & Exercise Medicine are merging to form a new College of Sport, Exercise & Musculoskeletal Medicine. I intend to build this fellowship around developing the knowledge, skills and experience required to make a valued and sustained contribution to the College, and to help drive the specialty into an era of greater impact as an independent, yet increasingly integrated, clinical and academic field of practice.

STRIKERS Elite Sport Group already provides research training for two PhD and four MD candidates, and this programme is one of several planned strands of its work, indexed here as Pillars of SEM.

Responsible AI in SEM

New and unfamiliar technology, including artificial intelligence and robot-assisted joint replacement, needs to be developed and appraised responsibly rather than adopted uncritically. This is itself an active focus of the group's work, engaging patients, advocates and regulators.

Reach beyond elite sport

Physical activity and exercise medicine services considered here extend to children, people with neurodevelopmental disability, and those living with frailty or multimorbidity, groups given less attention in a career weighted toward elite sport.

ND and NDD in children and young people

Neurodivergence (ND, including ADHD and autism) and neurodevelopmental disability (NDD, including intellectual disability and cerebral palsy) are considered across both the general population and sporting contexts. Musculoskeletal injury in this group is under-characterised, and the group's ND/NDD in MSK Injuries programme sits alongside this fellowship's musculoskeletal medicine domain rather than apart from it.

Digital and social media for adolescent health

Physical activity engagement through digital and social media, tested in an applied setting during the fellowship, is relevant to adolescents as much as to elite athletes, and the two audiences are treated as one line of enquiry.

Performance medicine

The clinical and academic practice covered here, athlete health systems, event care and rehabilitation across a career, is developed as part of a wider performance medicine research suite rather than as isolated attachments.

Sports medicine event care

Championship and community event medicine across the year builds toward a minimum dataset for event care surveillance and the Sports Medicine Event Care framework, the group's own research suite in this domain.

Outline

Dublin
Clinical and academic practice across general and performance populations; integrated surgical, medical and specialist rehabilitation care of upper and lower limb injury in elite sport; the training, governance and service development structures of a separately constituted Faculty. In 2027 Dublin hosts the 10th UEFA Medical Symposium.
Scotland
Sports event medicine at championship and community level; injury and illness surveillance at an IOC research centre; national clinical pathway design, and the governance and health economics of elective services; sports knee surgery, robot-assisted surgery, and sports traumatology including state of the art care in muscle and tendon injuries. In 2027 Scotland hosts the 155th Open at St Andrews, and the Edinburgh Orthopaedic & Sports Medicine Conference.
London
Leadership and education in elite sports medicine and surgery; sports knee surgery from elite performance to healthy active living; event medicine epidemiology and the older athlete; physical activity engagement through digital and social media, and cross-sector collaboration between health and social care, business and sport.
Berlin
Rehabilitation, strength and conditioning, and physical activity engagement delivered outside a health service.
